THE STATE VECTOR: 6 Numbers That Define a Reentry

PURPOSE
Introduce the mathematical structure of the simulation. Hook the engineers.

KEY POINTS
- State vector: [r, θ, φ, V, γ, ψ]
- Why spherical coordinates (not Cartesian)
- 3-DOF assumption: we track the vehicle's center of mass, not its rotation
- Getting one of these 6 numbers wrong can mean the difference between
  Pacific splashdown and atmospheric burnup

DRAFT HOOK
"You can describe where a spacecraft is and how fast it's going with just 6 numbers.
Getting those 6 numbers right is the difference between splashdown in the Pacific
and burning up in the atmosphere."

BODY BULLETS
  r   — radial distance from Earth's center [km] (not altitude — the difference matters)
  θ   — longitude, East positive [radians]
  φ   — latitude, North positive [radians]
  V   — velocity magnitude [km/s]
  γ   — flight path angle [radians] (positive = climbing, negative = diving)
  ψ   — heading angle [radians] (0 = North, 90° = East)

Explain the 3-DOF assumption: we're tracking where the vehicle goes and how fast,
not how it's tumbling. Good enough for trajectory-level fidelity.

POST 3 — Week 2, Monday
THE EQUATIONS OF MOTION: A System of 6 Coupled ODEs

PURPOSE
Show the actual governing equations. Demonstrate technical depth.
This is the post that earns credibility with aerospace professionals.

KEY POINTS
- 6 coupled, nonlinear ordinary differential equations
- Why "coupled": changing altitude changes density, which changes drag,
  which changes velocity, which changes heating — all simultaneously
- No analytical solution exists — must integrate numerically
- Bank angle σ is the ONLY control input — appears in only 2 of the 6 equations

DRAFT HOOK
"Here are the 6 equations that govern every atmospheric reentry ever flown.
They look simple. They are not."

EQUATIONS (render as clean image — use LaTeX or formatted text screenshot):

  dr/dt  =  V sin(γ)
  dθ/dt  =  V cos(γ) cos(ψ) / (r cos(φ))
  dφ/dt  =  V cos(γ) sin(ψ) / r
  dV/dt  = -a_drag - g sin(γ)
  dγ/dt  =  (1/V) [a_lift cos(σ) + (V²/r - g) cos(γ)]
  dψ/dt  =  (1/V) [a_lift sin(σ)/cos(γ) - (V²/r) cos(γ) cos(ψ) tan(φ)]

BODY
Walk through each equation physically. dr/dt is just "how fast is altitude changing."
dV/dt is drag slowing you down plus gravity pulling you deeper.
The interesting ones are dγ/dt and dψ/dt — that's where bank angle σ appears.
That's your only control lever. Everything else is physics.

POST 4 — Week 2, Thursday
THE ATMOSPHERE: One Equation to Model Everything

PURPOSE
Explain the atmospheric density model. Show the simplification tradeoff.

KEY POINTS
- Exponential density model: ρ(h) = ρ_s × exp(-β × (r - r_s))
  where ρ_s = 1.225 kg/m³, β = 0.14 km⁻¹, r_s = 6378.137 km
- Why density drops exponentially with altitude
- Above ~120 km: essentially vacuum — no meaningful drag
- Below ~50 km: density is high enough that drag is crushing
- The reentry corridor is the thin band between "skip out of the atmosphere"
  and "auger in and burn up" — roughly 120 km down to 40 km is where it all happens
- This model is simpler than US Standard Atmosphere 1976, but accurate enough
  for trajectory-level fidelity

DRAFT HOOK
"99% of Earth's atmosphere sits below 32 km.
Reentry vehicles travel through the other 1% faster than a rifle bullet.
Here's the single equation that models all of it."

BODY
Explain scale height intuition: for every 7 km you go up, density drops by roughly
half. At 120 km, density is about 10⁻⁸ times sea level. That's why spacecraft orbit
up there without significant drag. But as they dip below 80 km during reentry,
everything changes fast.

Note the deliberate simplification: a more complex atmosphere model would add
complexity without meaningfully changing the trajectory at the 3-DOF fidelity level.
The engineer's job is knowing which simplifications are acceptable.

POST 5 — Week 3, Monday
THE HARDEST PART: Aerodynamic Heating

PURPOSE
Explain heat flux. The most dramatic physics in the entire reentry problem.
This post will resonate with the widest audience — plasma is viscerally interesting.

KEY POINTS
- Sutton-Graves heat flux equation:
  Q = 199,830 × (ρ/ρ_sea)^0.5 × (V/V_sea)^3.15 × 1,000  [W/m²]
- The V^3.15 dependence — velocity matters enormously more than density
- This is why slowing down first (via skip maneuver) before going deep is so critical
- Peak heat flux does NOT occur at maximum velocity — it occurs later, when the
  vehicle is slower but the air is thick enough to matter
- The plasma sheath around an Apollo capsule reached ~7,000°C — hotter than
  the sun's photosphere (~5,500°C)
- Stagnation temperature also modeled: T_stag = T_∞ × [1 + (γ-1)/2 × Mach²]

DRAFT HOOK
"At 9 km/s, the air in front of your vehicle can't move fast enough to get out
of the way. It compresses. It heats. The plasma in front of an Apollo capsule
was hotter than the surface of the sun.
Here's the equation that predicts how much your heat shield needs to survive."

BODY
Walk through the physical intuition. Q scales with V^3.15 — which means doubling
your velocity multiplies heat flux by 8.8x. This is why the first few seconds of
reentry are so dangerous. As velocity decreases and altitude decreases, you hit a
crossover point where the density increase starts to win over the velocity decrease.
That's when heat flux peaks — roughly 40-60 km altitude.

Note: ablative heat shields work by absorbing heat through material phase change
(charring, melting, sublimating). They literally sacrifice themselves to keep the
vehicle cool. Pure insulation doesn't work — the heat rates are too high.

POST 6 — Week 3, Thursday
SOLVING IT: The Cash-Karp RK5 Method

PURPOSE
Explain numerical integration. Show computational rigor and software depth.

KEY POINTS
- No closed-form solution exists for the 6-DOF reentry equations
- Must integrate numerically — advance the solution one small time step at a time
- Euler's method: simple but unstable and inaccurate for stiff ODEs
- RK4: better — 4 stages per step, widely used
- Cash-Karp RK5: 6 stages, computes both 4th- and 5th-order solutions per step,
  uses the difference as an error estimate
- Time step: 0.1 seconds — ~2,500 steps per trajectory
- Runs in <1 second in the browser

DRAFT HOOK
"You can't solve these equations analytically. No closed form exists.
So I solved them numerically — stepping forward 0.1 seconds at a time,
25,000 computations per trajectory.
Here's the algorithm that makes it work."

CASH-KARP INTUITION
At each time step:
  1. Evaluate the derivatives at 6 different points (k1 through k6)
  2. Combine them in two different weighted averages:
     - A 4th-order estimate (less accurate)
     - A 5th-order estimate (more accurate)
  3. Use the 5th-order result to advance the solution
  4. The difference between 4th and 5th order estimates = local truncation error
     — a built-in accuracy sensor

This gives you accuracy AND an error estimate, without needing to run the
simulation twice.

6-stage RK5 structure showing how k1-k6 are combined.

POST 8 — Week 4, Thursday
THE SKIP-ENTRY MANEUVER: How Apollo Managed the Heat

PURPOSE
Explain the most counterintuitive concept in reentry. High emotional and
intellectual payoff — this is where the physics gets genuinely dramatic.

KEY POINTS
- A skip-entry uses lift to "bounce" off the upper atmosphere, extending
  the reentry corridor and reducing peak heat flux
- Bank angle is the control input that modulates lift direction
- Apollo 10's key maneuver: bank angle rolls from 0° to 180° at t=88-96 seconds
  — this is a full inversion, maximizing downward lift to push into the atmosphere
  for deceleration, then pull back out
- Effect: distributes thermal load over a longer time, reduces peak heat flux,
  increases landing accuracy
- Tradeoff: more time at high altitude = more total heat absorbed

BANK ANGLE INTUITION
  σ = 0°   → lift straight up → fights gravity → vehicle tends to skip out
  σ = 90°  → lift sideways → lateral maneuvering → no altitude change
  σ = 180° → lift straight down → pushed into atmosphere → maximum drag/decel
  Apollo 10 used the full range from -86° to 180°

DRAFT HOOK
"Apollo astronauts didn't just fall through the atmosphere. They flew through it.
A precisely timed roll maneuver let them 'skip' off the upper atmosphere like a
stone skipping on water.
Here's how — and why it was worth the risk."

BODY
Explain the tradeoff: a pure ballistic (no-lift) reentry has very high peak
deceleration and very high peak heat flux but is short. A skip-entry extends the
timeline and reduces peaks — but requires precise guidance. Apollo's guidance
computer had to execute this maneuver in real time with 1960s hardware.

Show the timeline: at t=88s the bank angle snaps to 180°. Watch what happens
to the altitude trace — the descent slows, almost stops, before resuming.
That's the "skip."

VISUAL
Animated GIF or side-by-side screenshot: bank angle chart at top, altitude chart
at bottom, with a vertical line showing t=88s where the reversal happens.
The altitude trace's change in slope at that moment tells the whole story.

CTA

PURPOSE
Connect the simulation to real engineering value. This is not just a demo —

KEY POINTS
- Every slider is a real design parameter:
    Mass (2,000-10,000 kg)

    Flight path angle (-20° to +5°)
    Lift-to-drag ratio (0-1.0)
    Drag coefficient (0.5-2.5)
    Reference area (5-50 m²)
- The fundamental reentry design tradeoff:
    Higher L/D → lower peak heat rate, better landing accuracy → more complex guidance
    Lower L/D → higher peak heat rate, less accurate → simpler guidance, smaller vehicle
- Apollo was designed at L/D ≈ 0.3 deliberately — not by default
- Mercury capsules: L/D ≈ 0 (ballistic) — simple guidance, brutal heating
- Space Shuttle: L/D ≈ 1.0 — complex guidance, gentle heating, massive vehicle

watch the heat flux spike and peak deceleration hit 10g+. Now set L/D = 0.5:
the heat rate drops, the trajectory extends, landing accuracy improves — but now
the guidance problem gets much harder.

Apollo chose L/D = 0.3 to get crew survivable g-forces (~6g peak), manageable
heat flux with ablative shield technology of the 1960s, and good enough landing
accuracy for Pacific recovery. Every number was a deliberate engineering trade.
