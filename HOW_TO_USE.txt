================================================================================
  MOAB REENTRY VIEWER — HOW TO USE
================================================================================

This tool simulates an object re-entering Earth's atmosphere and shows you
what happens to it in real time — altitude, speed, g-forces, heating, and
where it lands. There are two parts:

  1. The MAIN SIM   — where you set parameters and read charts (index.html)
  2. The 3D VIEWER  — a live 3D animation of the reentry on a globe

Open index.html in Chrome to get started.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  QUICK START
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. Open index.html in Chrome.
2. Move any slider — the charts update instantly.
3. Click "Launch 3-D Trajectory Visualization" to open the 3D globe window.
4. Press Play in the 3D window to watch the reentry unfold.

That's it. Everything else below is just detail on what each control does.


================================================================================
  PART 1 — THE MAIN SIM (index.html)
================================================================================

The left panel has all your inputs. The right side has all the output charts.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  VEHICLE SLIDERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

These three sliders control what your vehicle looks like and how it enters.
Every change instantly re-runs the simulation.

  Vehicle Mass (2,000 – 10,000 kg)
    How heavy the vehicle is. Heavier vehicles decelerate more slowly and
    tend to arrive lower in the atmosphere with more remaining speed.

  Entry Velocity (7 – 12 km/s)
    How fast the vehicle is going at the top of the atmosphere (~120 km).
    7.9 km/s is low Earth orbit speed. Values above ~11 km/s represent
    a vehicle coming back from the Moon. This is the single biggest driver
    of how hot and violent the reentry is.

  Flight Path Angle (-20° to +5°)
    The angle at which the vehicle enters, measured below horizontal.
    Shallower (near 0°) = gentler entry but risks skipping back out to space.
    Steeper (toward -20°) = more g-forces and much more heat.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  AERODYNAMICS SLIDERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

These control the shape and aerodynamic behavior of the vehicle.

  L/D — Lift-to-Drag Ratio (0 to 1)
    How much lift the vehicle generates relative to its drag.
    0 = a pure ballistic object (like a cannonball).
    0.3 = typical Apollo-style capsule.
    1 = a gliding vehicle like the Space Shuttle.
    Higher L/D = more cross-range control and a gentler landing approach.

  CD — Drag Coefficient (0.5 to 2.5)
    How "draggy" the vehicle's shape is.
    A blunt capsule is around 1.3. A sleeker shape is lower.
    Higher = more drag = slower descent.

  Reference Area (5 – 50 m²)
    The cross-sectional area of the vehicle that faces the airflow.
    Think of it as how big the vehicle's "face" is going in.
    Apollo CM was about 12 m².

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  BANK ANGLE — 5 MODES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Bank angle is how the vehicle rolls during reentry. It steers the lift
vector left, right, up, or down, and is the primary way pilots control
where a capsule or glider lands.

Click one of the five tab buttons to choose a mode:

  CONSTANT
    A single slider (0°–180°). The vehicle holds that exact bank angle for
    the whole flight. 0° = lift pointing straight up (maximum upward lift).
    90° = lift pointing sideways (maximum turning). 180° = lift pointing
    down (pushes vehicle into the atmosphere harder).

  PIECEWISE (Waypoints)
    Define a timeline of bank angles. Each row has a TIME (in seconds) and
    an ANGLE. The simulation smoothly interpolates between them.
    - Click "+ Add Waypoint" to add a new time/angle pair.
    - Click the red X on any row to delete it (minimum 2 rows required).
    - "Clear All Nodes" resets everything to a flat zero-degree profile.

  VISUAL (Drag-to-Draw)
    Click and drag the dots directly on the Bank Angle chart to reshape the
    bank profile by hand. Double-click a dot to delete it.
    "Clear All Nodes" resets to a flat line.

  CSV (Paste Data)
    Paste time/angle pairs into the text box, one pair per line, separated
    by a space. Example:
        0 0
        100 60
        200 45
        500 0
    Then click "Apply CSV Data" to load it.

  APOLLO 10
    Loads the actual bank angle profile flown by the Apollo 10 Command
    Module on May 26, 1969. Also automatically sets the vehicle mass,
    velocity, flight path angle, and aerodynamics to Apollo 10 values.
    A banner in the top-right corner confirms "APOLLO 10 MODE ACTIVE."
    The Altitude and Velocity charts will overlay real Apollo 10 flight
    data in gold dashed lines for comparison.
    Note: Moving any other slider will exit Apollo 10 mode.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  MANUAL ENTRY (✎ button)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Click "✎ Manual Entry" at the top of the left panel to open a dialog where
you can type exact numbers for every parameter at once. This is useful when
you want precision beyond what the sliders allow, or want to set entry
conditions not available on the main panel:

  Vehicle Properties:   Mass, CD (drag), L/D (lift), Reference Area
  Entry Conditions:     Velocity, Flight Path Angle, Entry Altitude,
                        Entry Heading (compass), Entry Latitude, Entry Longitude
  Simulation Settings:  Max Sim Time (how long the simulation runs in seconds)

Click "Apply & Run" to apply all values and re-run the simulation.
Click "Cancel" to close without changing anything.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  DISPLAY OPTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Show Peak Heat Flux Indicators (toggle switch)
    When turned on, two dashed lines appear on the charts:
    - An orange vertical line marks the moment of peak heating.
    - A blue horizontal line on the Altitude chart marks the altitude where
      that peak heating occurred.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  MISSION TARGET (Landing Zone)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

These three fields let you define a target landing zone on the ground.
Once set, a gold circle appears on the Ground Track chart.

  Target Lat    Latitude of the target center (decimal degrees)
  Target Lon    Longitude of the target center (decimal degrees)
  Corridor R    Radius of the landing zone circle (km)

After the simulation runs, the Ground Track chart will label the final
landing point as either "INSIDE CORRIDOR" (green) or "OUTSIDE CORRIDOR"
(red) based on whether it falls within that radius.

You can also set the target by clicking directly on the globe in the
3D Viewer (see the P key under Part 2).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  DESIGN CONSTRAINTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

These two fields draw red limit lines on the relevant charts and turn the
summary metric cards red if the simulation exceeds your limits.

  Max Decel     Maximum allowed deceleration, in g's.
                Draws a red dashed line on the Deceleration chart.

  Max Heat Flux Maximum allowed heating rate, in kW/m².
                Draws a red dashed line on the Heat Flux chart.

Leave either field blank (or type "off") to disable that constraint.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TOOLBAR BUTTONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

These appear below the left panel.

  🚀 Launch 3-D Trajectory Visualization
    Opens the 3D globe viewer in a new window. Run a simulation first.
    The trajectory data streams automatically to the 3D window.

  ◀◀  and  ▶▶
    Slow down or speed up the animated playback dot that moves across
    all the charts in sync with the 3D animation.

  ⬇ Export Telemetry XLSX
    Downloads a full Excel workbook with two sheets:
    - Vehicle Data: your inputs and peak summary results
    - Time History: complete second-by-second data (altitude, velocity,
      deceleration, heat flux, flight path angle, bank angle, lat/lon,
      downrange, crossrange)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  CHARTS (right side, scrollable)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Nine charts update every time you move a slider. You can scroll down to
see them all. A moving dot (magenta) tracks the 3D animation across
every chart in real time.

  1. Bank Angle vs Time      Your commanded bank angle over the flight
  2. Altitude vs Time        How high the vehicle is (km)
  3. Velocity vs Time        How fast it's going (km/s)
  4. Deceleration vs Time    G-forces experienced
  5. Heat Flux vs Time       Heating rate on the heat shield (kW/m²)
  6. Flight Path Angle vs Time  Entry angle changing over time
  7. Altitude vs Downrange   How far ahead the vehicle gets as it descends
  8. Altitude vs Crossrange  How far sideways it drifts as it descends
  9. Ground Track (Lat/Lon)  Map view of the trajectory path and landing point

The Bank Angle chart has two small buttons in its title bar:
  ±90  — shows the Y axis from -90° to +90°
  ±180 — shows the Y axis from -180° to +180° (default)

The summary cards at the bottom of the left panel show:
  Max Deceleration   Peak heat flux altitude   Peak g-load   Peak stagnation temp


================================================================================
  PART 2 — THE 3D REENTRY VISUALIZER
================================================================================

Click "Launch 3-D Trajectory Visualization" in the main sim to open this
window. It shows your vehicle flying through the atmosphere on an interactive
3D Earth.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  BUTTONS (top bar)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  ▶ Play Animation / ⏸ Pause
    Starts or pauses the animation. The vehicle (shown as a 3D model) travels
    along the trajectory. The HUD, gauges, and chart dots all update live.

  🌍 Global Mode  (keyboard: G)
    Free-orbit view. You can spin the Earth, zoom in and out, see the full
    trajectory arc from any angle. Best for an overview of the flight path.

  🛸 Trajectory Mode  (keyboard: T)
    The camera rides along with the vehicle. Good for a "ride along" view.
    Automatically enables Vehicle Lock when you switch to this mode.

  🔒 Vehicle Lock  (keyboard: V)
    Only available in Trajectory Mode. Keeps the camera locked just behind
    the vehicle in a chase-camera view. Press V again to unlock and orbit
    freely around the vehicle's current position.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  MOUSE CONTROLS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Click + Drag          Rotate the view / orbit the Earth
  Scroll Wheel          Zoom in and out
  Shift + Scroll        Move the camera pivot point along the trajectory
                        (useful in Trajectory Mode to look ahead or behind)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  KEYBOARD SHORTCUTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Spacebar         Play or pause the animation
  G                Switch to Global Mode
  T                Switch to Trajectory Mode (also enables Vehicle Lock)
  V                Toggle Vehicle Lock on/off (Trajectory Mode only)
  H                Toggle Horizon Lock (Trajectory Mode only)
                   Keeps the horizon level in the chase camera view
  Arrow Keys       Rotate the camera (pan around the view)
  + or =           Zoom in
  - or _           Zoom out
  . or >           Speed up time warp (animation plays faster)
  , or <           Slow down time warp (animation plays slower)
  [ (left bracket) Jump camera pivot backward 10 steps along the trajectory
  ] (right bracket) Jump camera pivot forward 10 steps along the trajectory
  P                Toggle Target Placement Mode (see below)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  PLACING A LANDING TARGET IN THE 3D VIEWER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Press P (or use the P key shown in the CONTROLS panel) to enter Target
Placement Mode. The cursor changes to a crosshair.

  - Click anywhere on the Earth's surface to drop the landing zone center
    at that lat/lon.
  - A gold circle and dot will appear showing the target zone.
  - Drag the white handle on the ring edge to resize the radius.

The latitude, longitude, and radius automatically update in the main sim's
Target Lat / Target Lon / Corridor R fields. Press P again to exit
placement mode.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  ON-SCREEN DISPLAYS (3D VIEWER)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  HUD (top-right corner)
    A fighter-jet-style display showing live telemetry as the animation plays:
    Altitude, Velocity, Mach number, G-load, Flight Path Angle, Bank Angle,
    Latitude, Longitude, and current time in seconds.

  Heat Flux Gauge (right side, below HUD)
    An arc meter showing current heating rate (kW/m²). The needle tracks
    the current value. A lighter "peak needle" holds the maximum recorded
    during this animation run.

  Deceleration Gauge (right side, below Heat Flux)
    Same style as the Heat Flux gauge. Shows current and peak g-forces.

  Time Warp Indicator (top-left corner)
    Shows the current animation speed multiplier, e.g., "x10" or "x100".

  Vehicle Viewer Pane (bottom-left corner)
    A close-up 3D view of the vehicle model, showing its orientation and
    heat shield state. The model automatically matches your L/D setting
    (e.g., Apollo capsule, Space Shuttle, X-37B, etc.)

  CONTROLS Panel (top-left)
    A collapsible reference list of all keyboard shortcuts.
    Click the "CONTROLS" title bar to expand or collapse it.


================================================================================
  TIPS
================================================================================

- The simulation runs automatically every time you move a slider. You don't
  need to click a "Run" button.

- Apollo 10 mode is a great reference point. Load it, look at the charts,
  then tweak sliders to see how small changes would have changed the outcome.

- If the vehicle "skips out" (never lands), try making the flight path angle
  a little steeper (more negative).

- Use the 3D viewer's Trajectory Mode + Vehicle Lock for the best sense of
  what it would feel like to be on board during reentry.

- Export to XLSX if you want to do your own analysis in Excel or another tool.
